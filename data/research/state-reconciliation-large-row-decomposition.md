# FY2024 reconciliation rows at or above $5 billion

Updated August 29, 2026.

## Outcome

The browser-path audit now treats $5 billion as the hard review threshold. Every visible row at or above that amount is either:

1. an exact additive breakdown tied to the same official source and accounting basis; or
2. a visibly labeled source ceiling explaining why a supported lower-level allocation is not attached.

All 50 audited GAAP primary-government expense controls have been replaced by official Statement of Activities function schedules. No state GAAP total remains as one standalone control row.

The automated audit finds 483 large row occurrences across the two signed reconciliation bridges:

| GAAP functions | 120 | 19 | 101 |
| Census functions | 264 | 88 | 176 |
| Official archive | 99 | 34 | 65 |

Census functions occur twice because the browser shows them with opposite signs in both the archive-to-Census and Census-to-GAAP bridges. The counts are browser occurrences, not unique financial accounts.

## Accounting and privacy guardrails

- A child schedule is accepted only when its fiscal period, entity boundary, measure, sign, and source precision match the parent.
- Child rows must reproduce the parent in integer cents; rounded ACFR schedules retain only explicit rounding bridges.
- Census, GAAP, budget, and payment/checkbook layers are not mixed to force a value below the threshold.
- Person-level recipient names are not reconstructed from redacted or ambiguous public-payee groups.
- A source ceiling is evidence of the publication/import boundary, not a claim that the economic activity is indivisible.

Census remains a standardized public-use product whose classifications and government boundary can differ from a state's own records, so unmatched state checkbook detail is not treated as a Census decomposition. [Census FY2024 technical documentation](https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/state/technical-documentation/complete-technical-documentation/statetechdoc2024.pdf) · [Census state-finance methodology](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/state/technical-documentation/methodology.html)
